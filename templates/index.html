Who we are

The Jonuel Entrepreneurship Foundation (JEF) is a Zambian youth-led nonprofit organisation based in Mongu, Western Province. We exist to transform the lives of youth, women and persons with disabilities through entrepreneurship, skills development, agriculture, governance, gender justice advocacy and innovation.

We believe that even if we cannot change the whole world, we can change the environment around us, by nurturing empowered, self-reliant and community-driven young people who become the architects of their own futures.

What we do

Skills and Entrepreneurship

Practical, market-ready skills and incubation support that help young people create their own income.

Governance and Civic Engagement

Civic literacy and youth voices in decision-making, for transparent and accountable leadership.

Gender Justice and SRH

Equal rights, safe spaces and accurate health information for young women, girls and persons with disabilities.

Climate Change and Agriculture

Climate-smart farming and agribusiness that build food security and resilient livelihoods.

Mental Health and Psychosocial Support

Peer support and awareness that help young people build emotional resilience.

Featured initiative

Financial Awareness and Financial Literacy

Through collaboration with The Financial Awareness Foundation (TFAF) and the Centre for Financial Literacy Education Africa (CFLE-Africa), JEF supports practical financial education for young people, families, entrepreneurs and communities. Activities include the April Financial Literacy Month campaign, October Estate & Gift Planning Awareness Month, educational webinars, digital resources and community engagement.

Featured programme

The YESDF Fellowship

Youth Empowerment and Skills Development Fellowship. Empowered to Lead, Skilled to Succeed.

YESDF is JEF’s flagship fellowship, delivered with the Department of Youth, Western Province. Each year, 200 young people aged 18 to 35 join an intensive boot camp in entrepreneurship, leadership, integrity and practical skills, followed by six months of mentorship.

The challenge we are responding to

Western Province faces a convergence of challenges that leave young people, women and persons with disabilities behind.

Limited vocational training

Accessible technical and vocational institutions are scarce. Skills such as tailoring, agriculture and digital technology are out of reach for many because of cost, distance or lack of awareness.

High youth unemployment

Youth unemployment among those aged 15 to 35 exceeds 24% nationally (ZamStats, 2022), and Western Province is consistently among the most affected regions.

Poverty and exclusion

Over 82% of Western Province’s population lives below the poverty line. Women, youth and persons with disabilities face compounded vulnerability.

Our approach

1. Practical Skills Training

Hands-on training in agriculture, tailoring and fashion design, digital skills and entrepreneurship.

2. Mentorship and Incubation

Personal mentorship, peer networks and business support to turn ideas into viable enterprises.

3. Community Outreach and Advocacy

Civic education and community engagement that mobilise participation and advocate for change.

4. Institutional Collaboration

Partnerships with government, schools, colleges, NGOs and development agencies to widen our reach.
