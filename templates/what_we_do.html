JEF’s work is organised around five thematic areas that address the most pressing challenges facing young people and marginalised groups in Zambia. Each area is grounded in evidence, community need and a commitment to inclusive development.

1. Skills and Entrepreneurship Empowerment

Youth unemployment and a lack of vocational training are among the most serious barriers to development in Zambia. JEF equips young people, especially those from under-privileged backgrounds, with practical, market-relevant skills in agriculture, tailoring, digital technology and business development. By nurturing an entrepreneurial mindset early and providing incubation support, we enable young people to create self-employment, earn an income and contribute to their local economies, building a generation that is economically resilient.

2. Governance, Civic Advocacy and Engagement

Democratic participation and accountability are foundational to national development. Many young Zambians feel excluded from governance and lack the knowledge or platform to engage with public institutions. JEF builds civic literacy, promotes youth participation in decision-making and advocates for transparent and accountable leadership. Through community engagement, partnerships with government institutions and anti-corruption advocacy, we help young people become active citizens who hold leaders accountable.

3. Gender Justice and Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH)

Gender inequality and limited access to SRH information continue to affect young women, girls and persons with disabilities. JEF promotes equal rights, challenges harmful cultural norms and provides safe spaces where young people can access accurate health information. Our work includes awareness campaigns, community dialogues and capacity-building sessions on gender-based violence, early marriage, menstrual health and reproductive rights.

4. Climate Change and Agriculture

Western Province is one of Zambia’s most climate-vulnerable regions, and communities depend heavily on rain-fed agriculture. JEF builds climate literacy among young people and trains them in climate-smart, sustainable farming, including agribusiness, fresh produce cultivation and adaptive agriculture. By turning young people into informed agripreneurs and environmental stewards, we contribute to food security and long-term ecological sustainability.

5. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support

The mental health burden among Zambian youth is growing, yet it remains stigmatised and largely unaddressed. Unemployment, poverty, social exclusion and trauma contribute to anxiety, depression and substance abuse. JEF integrates mental health awareness and psychosocial support into its programmes. We offer tools for emotional resilience, self-awareness and coping, through peer support networks, awareness sessions and partnerships with health professionals.

Our programmes and accomplishments

Agriculture and Agribusiness Development

Training in climate-smart agriculture, backyard gardening, poultry production and agribusiness. Participants have gone on to start their own farming ventures and cooperative groups, improving household food security and local economic activity.

Digital Literacy and Technology Skills

Training in basic computing, Microsoft Office, graphic design, digital marketing and data entry. Graduates are competing in the digital economy, with many securing employment or freelance work.

Tailoring, Fashion Design and Garment Production

Practical skills in tailoring, cutting and fashion design. Many graduates have started tailoring businesses or joined cooperatives, with particular impact for young women who had few economic opportunities.

Entrepreneurship Skills Fairs and Summits

Fairs, youth summits and capacity-building workshops that connect young people with mentors, market opportunities and business development resources.

Gender Justice and SRH Advocacy

Community campaigns, dialogues and training that address gender-based violence, early marriage and reproductive health, shifting attitudes among community members and traditional leaders.

Governance, Civic Education and Institutional Engagement

Work with government institutions, civil society and traditional leadership, including partnerships with the Ministry of Youth, Sport and Arts, positioning young people as credible agents of change.

Financial Awareness and Financial Literacy

JEF advances financial awareness and financial literacy in collaboration with The Financial Awareness Foundation (TFAF) and the Centre for Financial Literacy Education Africa (CFLE-Africa). The programme equips young people, families, entrepreneurs and communities with practical knowledge to make informed money decisions, develop responsible financial habits, and plan for the future.

The work includes the April Financial Literacy Month campaign, October Estate & Gift Planning Awareness Month, educational webinars, digital resources, community activities, and the development of Financial Awareness Associations in universities and colleges.
