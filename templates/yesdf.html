Overview

YESDF is a locally anchored fellowship that prepares young people for life, work and leadership. It is presented by the Jonuel Entrepreneurship Foundation in partnership with the Department of Youth, Western Province, with endorsement from the Provincial Administration.

More than a training, YESDF is a movement to change the story of young people in Western Province, from recipients of aid to drivers of development.

Why YESDF

Western Province is one of Zambia’s least industrially developed provinces. Most people depend on small-scale agriculture and informal trade. Young people aged 18 to 35 are the largest group, yet they face high unemployment, limited entrepreneurship opportunities, early school leaving and substance abuse.

Every year, thousands of Grade 12 school leavers finish school with little prospect of further training or work, which can lead to frustration, social vices and crime. YESDF offers a productive, hopeful alternative.

The fellowship was inspired by the Founder’s experience in the Mandela Washington Fellowship 2025.

Our goal

To empower and equip young people in Western Province with entrepreneurial, leadership and life skills that foster self-reliance, integrity and innovation, so they become agents of positive change and contributors to sustainable community development.

Vision

A Western Province where every young person is skilled, productive and purpose-driven, transforming potential into impact and creating pathways for inclusive economic and social development.

Mission

To nurture a generation of empowered, ethical and innovative young Zambians through practical training, mentorship and institutional collaboration that bridges the gap between education, employment and enterprise creation.

Our objectives

  • Provide intensive skills and entrepreneurship training for young people aged 18 to 35
  • Promote integrity and civic responsibility
  • Strengthen partnerships between youth, government, the private sector and traditional leaders
  • Reduce youth idleness and involvement in crime by providing productive alternatives
  • Create a mentorship and networking platform linking young people to civic, religious and traditional leaders

The YESDF Programme

  1. Step 1: Launch Summit

    “Dare to Invent the Future Youth Summit”
    Theme: STEP UP! Young People against Corruption and Moral Decay: Shaping the Current and Future of Bulozi.

    The summit officially launches each fellowship edition. It brings government departments, the private sector, NGOs, civil society, churches, traditional leaders and youth representatives together for dialogue on integrity, leadership and innovation.

  2. Step 2: Two-Week Empowerment Boot Camp

    Every December, 200 youth fellows take part in a two-week boot camp at public education institutions in Mongu. Learning is practical, through workshops, interactive sessions, team projects and field visits.

    Module 1: Entrepreneurship and Business Innovation. Business model development, access to finance and SME linkages, cooperative formation, financial literacy.

    Module 2: Leadership and Integrity. Ethics in leadership, civic responsibility and governance, anti-corruption awareness, moral uprightness.

    Module 3: Digital and Vocational Skills. ICT for business; tailoring, carpentry, crafts and agribusiness basics.

    Module 4: Personal and Social Development. Communication and teamwork, mental health and emotional intelligence, gender equality and inclusion.

    Module 5: Career and Community Engagement. Volunteering and mentorship, community service project design, networking and professional growth.

  3. Step 3: Industrial and Institutional Tours

    Fellows visit local industries, government departments and agricultural enterprises to see real workplaces and systems at first hand. The tours strengthen career orientation, build understanding of professional standards and work ethics, and create relationships for future collaboration.

  4. Step 4: Mentorship and Follow-Up

    After the boot camp, fellows receive six months of follow-up mentorship, complete individual development plans and join the alumni network.

Apply to YESDF: Eligibility and Selection

Who we are looking for

YESDF welcomes dynamic young people who show a strong commitment to personal growth, leadership, entrepreneurship and community development. Each year we select 200 fellows:

100 fellows (50%)

Grade 12 school leavers

60 fellows (30%)

College and university students or graduates

40 fellows (20%)

Young people from communities, faith groups and the informal sector, including local entrepreneurs

Participants come from both rural and urban areas of Western Province.

Eligibility checklist

To be considered, you must:

  • Be a Zambian citizen living in Western Province
  • Be between 18 and 35 years old
  • Show interest or involvement in entrepreneurship, leadership or community service
  • Have at least a Grade 12 education or equivalent
  • Be willing and available to attend the full two-week boot camp in December
  • Show discipline, integrity and teamwork
  • Be recommended by a recognised institution, school administration, youth organisation, community leader or religious leader, as applicable
  • Agree to pay the participant commitment fee

Gender and inclusion

YESDF is committed to gender equality and social inclusion. At least 50% of fellows will be young women. We prioritise young people living with disabilities, those from rural and marginalised communities and those from low-income households.

This approach is aligned with Zambia’s National Youth Policy, Vision 2030 and the UN Sustainable Development Goals 4, 5, 8, 10 and 16.

How selection works

  1. Step 1: Nomination and application. School administrations, through the DEBS and PEO offices, and community and youth organisations nominate deserving candidates. An open call for applications is announced through the media, social media and local institutions.
  2. Step 2: Screening and shortlisting. A selection committee made up of JEF, the Department of Youth and the Ministry of Education reviews all applications. Priority goes to applicants from vulnerable backgrounds and those who show innovation potential.
  3. Step 3: Final selection and notification. JEF and the Department of Youth jointly approve the final list. Selected fellows receive an official acceptance letter and programme guidelines.

Participant commitment fee

Each fellow contributes a commitment fee of K300. This builds ownership, accountability and discipline, and helps cover learning materials, certificates and identification cards.

Fellowship code of conduct

All fellows agree to:

  • Observe punctuality, discipline and respect throughout the programme
  • Abstain from drug or substance abuse, indecent behaviour and political interference
  • Take part actively in all sessions, field visits and mentorship activities
  • Represent their communities and institutions with integrity
  • Contribute to post-programme activities and the alumni network

Non-compliance may result in dismissal from the fellowship.

Benefits and outcomes

What fellows receive

  • Entrepreneurial and leadership skills that match today’s job market
  • Exposure to institutions, industries and farms through study visits
  • Networks and mentorship from public and private sector leaders
  • A certificate of completion endorsed by the Provincial Administration
  • Eligibility for future incubation, grants and mentorship programmes under JEF and its partners
  • Automatic membership of the Western Province Youth Empowerment and Skills Alumni Network

What we expect to achieve

  • Empowered youth with practical skills and better employability
  • More youth-led enterprises and innovations
  • Stronger moral integrity and civic participation
  • Fewer young people involved in social vices
  • Stronger collaboration between government and youth networks

Alumni network

Every fellow who completes YESDF joins the Western Province Youth Empowerment and Skills Alumni Network, a growing movement of empowered young people driving sustainable development, civic integrity and innovation in their communities.

Alumni mentor new cohorts, take part in follow-up activities and keep access to future incubation, grant and mentorship opportunities. YESDF runs every December, so the network grows each year.
